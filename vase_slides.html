Visual Question Answering in Clinical Workflows

1. Clinical Utility

Provides critical support to diagnostic workflows. Assistants answer natural questions on complex images like MRIs, CT scans, and X-Rays, improving radiologist and pathologist throughput.

2. The Generative Shift

Transition from discriminative classification to generative, LLM-backed multimodal decoders. These models describe complex visual relationships and logical paths in natural language.

3. The Safety Imperative

Clinical decision-making operates on a near-zero error tolerance. Traditional generative assistants often hallucinate, introducing severe diagnostic hazards.

Why do Medical VLMs Hallucinate?

  • Linguistic Prior Bias: VLMs over-rely on strong language priors learned during training, guessing answers based on text associations.
  • Visual Neglect: Decoders generate answers autoregressively, bypassing visual token mappings when questions contain highly correlated keywords.
  • Overconfidence: Hallucinated answers are outputted with high confidence scores, misleading clinicians and evaluation benchmarks.

Limitations of Current Mitigation Methods

Method Category Technical Approach Core Limitations in Medicine
Standard Semantic Entropy Measures linguistic entropy over generated text clusters. Cannot distinguish between textual uncertainty and visual misalignment.
Monte Carlo Dropout Runs inference repeatedly with active dropout nodes. Extremely high computational latency at inference time.
Instruction Fine-Tuning Tunes weights on curated negative/contrastive data. Expensive expert annotation requirements; risk of catastrophic forgetting.

The VASE Framework: Visual Grounding via Entropy

The VASE Principle

Instead of relying on textual confidence, VASE introduces an active visual intervention: measuring the change in semantic entropy when visual information is altered. Authentic grounding yields high sensitivity to visual changes; hallucinated language priors remain unaffected.

1. Training-Free

No model parameter modifications or retraining necessary. Integrates with frozen weights.

2. Contrastive

Compares original decoding probabilities against visual-intervention passes.

3. Calibration-Aligned

Aligns confidence with visual reality, supporting clinical selective prediction.

How Visual Perturbation Works

  • Local Obfuscation: Apply a mild visual mask, Gaussian noise, or patch dropout to the input scan.
  • Pathology Blurring: The intervention weakens fine-grained visual details while leaving the general spatial shape recognizable.
  • Semantic Decoupling: Isolates if the text generation output depends on these visual details or on the text question.

The VASE Dual-Pass Workflow

How Grounding is Quantified

1

Measure Baseline Output Uncertainty

Analyze how uncertain the model is about its answer given the original, unmodified image. We group output answers into semantic clusters to measure text output entropy.

2

Measure Perturbed Output Uncertainty

Blur or mask the visual abnormalities in the image, keep the text question identical, and calculate the uncertainty score again.

3

Compute the Visual Grounding Score

Uncertainty Contrast: Subtract the baseline uncertainty from the perturbed uncertainty. If blurring details causes uncertainty to spike, the model relies on the image (Grounded). If uncertainty remains unchanged, the model is guessing from text priors (Hallucinated).

Deciding When to Trust the Model

Trust Domain (High Uncertainty Contrast)

The visual mask successfully disrupted the model's prediction confidence.

Result: Accept and output VLM answer.

Hallucination Warning (Low Uncertainty Contrast)

Blurring the scan details had zero effect on the output. The model is guessing from language priors.

Result: Flag as hallucination / Abstain.

Selective Answering & Trust Routing

  • Selective Predict Policy: The clinical model will abstain and issue safety flags when visual confidence is ungrounded.
  • Risk-Coverage Tradeoff: Reducing coverage (answering fewer questions) significantly drives down clinical diagnostics risk.
  • VASE Superiority: Achieves a steeper drop in risk at comparable coverage levels, proving well-calibrated clinical trust.

Summary & Future Directions

  • Core Takeaway: Visual perturbation is a simple but highly effective training-free tool for verifying factual grounding.
  • Limitations: Dual forward passes increase latency. Highly sensitive to modality-specific masking patterns.
  • Future Integration: Future research involves using VASE entropy contrast signals directly as RL rewards (similar to DeepSeek-R1) to train models to naturally avoid language prior shortcuts.

RL Reward Extension Paradigm
